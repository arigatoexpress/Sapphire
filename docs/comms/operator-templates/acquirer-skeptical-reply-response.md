## Notes for the operator (NOT in the sent message)

- Why this works: acknowledges the pushback without folding ("fair on its face"), names the actual objection back to them in your own words (signals you read carefully), gives them an artifact to refute or accept, and offers the "what would have changed your mind" prompt as graceful exit.
- Common edits: paraphrase the specific objection accurately. If they said "your trading numbers aren't audited" don't paraphrase as "you don't trust our metrics" — that's a cheap rephrase. Use their words.
- Tone calibration: the operator's natural voice on this is to over-defend and add too much evidence. Resist. Three points, max one sentence per acknowledgement, then the offer. If they wanted a deck they'd have asked.
- DO NOT apologize for the price or the solo-engineer fact. Apologetic responses to corp-dev pushback are read as weakness and lower the offer.
- Do NOT accept the framing "we already have this internally" without pushing once. Most companies who say this have a partial version that solves a different problem; the diligence packet's `02-product-surfaces.md` is the artifact to point at.
