## Diligence Readout

The fastest way to evaluate Sapphire is to open `/showcase`, then ask whether every linked page has a safety stance. The answer should be visible: pages that inspect state should read as read-only, pages that draft actions should label them as drafts, and production-adjacent pages should point to confirmation, risk, or runbook controls. The strongest pages for a first acquirer demo are `/showcase`, `/diligence`, `/production-readiness`, `/sovereign-thesis`, `/security/overview`, `/risk`, `/investment-intel`, and `/agents/autonomous`. They show the system as an operating cockpit rather than a marketing site.

The second product question is whether the APIs are customer-grade. The inference proxy is the cleanest yes: quota, cache stats, tier headers, and OpenAI-compatible responses make it understandable to external consumers. The dashboard APIs are more internal but still useful: continuous-intelligence plan, artifact status, lease preview, Foundry readiness, Gemini OODA, and investment thesis routes are already composed around product concepts. The next commercial step would be to separate operator-only endpoints from buyer-facing demo endpoints and document auth/scopes for each.

The Hermes/plugin surface is useful because it prevents tool sprawl. Many AI-agent systems fail by making every script callable by the model. Sapphire has a registry, an agent-facing subset, and side-effect classification. That is a product decision, not just hygiene. It lets a buyer reason about which capabilities are safe to expose to support teams, which are internal routines, and which should remain behind human confirmation.

Screenshots are intentionally deferred because the packet should not fake UI polish. The placeholder files under `/Users/aribs/Code/Sapphire/docs/diligence/screenshots/` say exactly what to capture next. That is buyer-friendly: it separates implemented surfaces from presentation assets and makes the next diligence pass mechanical.

The commercial packaging opportunity is to split surfaces by buyer persona. A corp-dev reader gets the diligence packet and production-readiness page. A platform engineer gets inference quotas, cache stats, event bus, provenance verify, and Foundry sync. A risk leader gets the Risk Kernel quickstart, confirmation firewall, kill switches, and incident runbook. An operator gets the dashboard, Hermes skills, and daily OODA delta. The `/showcase` page now provides this split directly: friend, buyer, engineer, and operator demo paths are linked from one authenticated front door.

The main surface risk is density. A 43-route dashboard can overwhelm a buyer if the first experience is not curated. The product should therefore keep the dashboard dense for Ari while using `/showcase` as the small buyer-demo path: executive summary, readiness, risk, provenance, OODA, regional intelligence, and one intelligence artifact.
